## Content: Reflections (selected activities)

These are the three activities selected for the formal portfolio. Use these for any "reflection" or "about this project" sections.

### BSides Limburg

What makes BSides different from school seminars is the room. Everyone there chose to be there. The OT security talk stuck with me most — not because of new information, but because it sharpened something I already knew: good security is context-dependent. You can't apply modern tooling to legacy environments without first understanding how those systems actually work. That's how I approach problems generally — understand the environment before building a solution.

Being there as a Black Talon intern also felt different from being there as a student. I had context for what was being discussed. I could contribute to conversations instead of just listening. That shift matters.

### Studiereis Duitsland / Berlin

This trip was the first time in four years I felt like I was actually living rather than just grinding through a degree. That's not a complaint about the degree — it's just true that I'd been so focused on studying and building a life in a new country that I forgot to do the living part.

The CCC visit connected directly to what I care about professionally: digital rights, privacy as a technical and political problem, the responsibility that comes with working in security. The day after — just walking through East Berlin with an old friend — had nothing to do with IT and was probably the most meaningful part of the whole trip.

I came back with one concrete intention: be outside more. Go to more events. Get the camera out. Not as goals on a list — just as things I actually want to do.

### Innovatieroute Security & Cloud

The SD-WAN day was familiar territory from my ISP background — useful to see it in an enterprise context. The incident drill was the more valuable part. It forced me to think about security communication: when do you have to disclose a breach? How do you write a public statement that's honest but doesn't cause panic? How do you keep internal lines clear under pressure?

And I had to do all of that in Dutch. Which is my third language. That was uncomfortable and useful in equal measure. A real incident doesn't let you pick the language.

The takeaway: security professionals need to be good communicators. That's something I'm actively working on.
